Five ways Crumbs could look

Each direction shows the same three jobs, browsing the library, reading one recipe, and adding or editing a recipe, on a phone and on a desktop. The sample recipes reflect the current data model: ingredients carry a source and a percentage, steps carry a timing, and notes carry a date.

3 phone screens per direction 2 desktop screens per direction Static mockups, photos shown as placeholders
Direction 1

Rolodex

Cormorant italic, Quicksand, Caveat for notes

Why

Carries the existing mockup forward. Every recipe is a ruled index card in a box, so the app feels like a family collection rather than a database. Notes live on the back of the card in handwriting.

Tradeoff

Skeuomorphic and low density. A card holds one recipe at a time, so the library is browsed by flipping rather than scanning, and long ingredient lists fight the card metaphor.

Direction 2

Ledger

Hanken Grotesk, IBM Plex Mono for every number

Why

Brutally minimal: one paper tone, one ink, hairlines and type. Amounts, timings and percentages line up in a monospaced column, so a recipe reads like a well-kept ledger and the library scans in seconds. Trivial to theme.

Tradeoff

Can feel cold and administrative. Without photos or color there is little warmth, so the direction leans entirely on typographic discipline and the quality of the copy.

Direction 3

Editorial

DM Serif Display, Source Sans 3

Why

Every recipe is a magazine feature: a full-bleed photograph, a serif headline, a byline built from author and date, and notes set as pull-quotes in the margin. Recipes feel worth keeping and worth sharing.

Tradeoff

Lives or dies on photography. Recipes without a photo get a flat placeholder, and the generous spacing means fewer recipes per screen than any other direction.

Direction 4

Kitchen Console

Barlow Semi Condensed, JetBrains Mono for weights, timers and percentages

Why

Built for a phone propped on the counter: dark ground, one amber accent, 56px controls that work with wet hands. Uses the data model directly: every step's timing becomes a timer, every ingredient's percentage becomes a scaling column.

Tradeoff

Reads as equipment, not a cookbook. Dark-only tone can feel severe for browsing on the couch, and the density asks for careful typography to stay legible.

Direction 5

Retro Cookbook

Zilla Slab, Karla

Why

A 1970s community cookbook: mustard, olive and brick blocks on cream, a slab serif, dotted leaders between ingredient and amount, numbered circles for steps, and a "from the kitchen of" byline for the author. Instantly recognizable and warm without being precious.

Tradeoff

The novelty can wear over daily use, the three-color palette limits how much state you can encode, and the chunky type takes room that long ingredient lists need.
